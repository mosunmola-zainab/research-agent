# Full Paper Analysis

I want the analysis to make a research paper understandable to a layman. It should keep things simple without changing what the research actually says or dumbing it down.

## The Quickies

The analysis starts with the part a layman or someone with a short attention span can read and still understand the paper:

- What is this about?
- Why does it matter?
- What did we learn?
- Why should I care?
- What could this mean in the real world?
- What are the few things worth remembering?

This part should be short. Someone who wants more can continue into the deeper analysis.

## Going Deeper

The deeper analysis explains the main concepts first, then walks through the paper's main ideas. Technical terms are explained as they come up instead of assuming the reader already knows them.

How deep it goes depends on the paper. It could cover the methodology, architecture, experiments, evidence, benchmarks, results applications, limitations, or research direction. A review paper and an experimental paper do not need the exact same analysis.

Important numbers and evidence are explained where they come up, with the main results and numbers also collected somewhere it is easy to come back to.

If a figure, table, or diagram matters, the analysis explains what it shows and how to read it. It should also point out when it is worth going back to the original. If it is overly complicated, it can give a simpler version.

For equations, I mostly care about what they mean and why they matter.

## Reading the Research Carefully

It should be clear when something is:

- the authors' own work or claim
- something they are reporting from another study
- from outside research
- the agent's own interpretation

The analysis should not make a claim stronger than the evidence supports. Important conditions, baselines, uncertainty, and other details that change how a result should be understood should not disappear during explanation.

It should also explain what the paper does well and where it falls short. The authors' own limitations should be kept separate from problems the agent notices itself.

If the paper does not answer something, the analysis should say so. It can explain what is missing, offer an interpretation, or look outside the paper when that helps, but it should be clear which one it is doing.

## Applications and Bigger Picture

Applications should be explained in terms of what they could actually look like in real life. The analysis should separate what already exists from what is proposed, experimental, or speculative. It can suggest other reasonable applications, but those should be clearly identified as the agent's own inference.

The paper can also be connected to other research where there is a useful connection. If it connects to something I am working on, the analysis should explain what I could actually do with that connection.

New research questions or experiments only need to come up when the paper actually opens up something interesting.

## Before I Leave

The analysis ends with 3–5 things worth remembering, who the paper is useful for, whether it is worth reading, skimming, or skipping, and which parts are worth reading in the original.
